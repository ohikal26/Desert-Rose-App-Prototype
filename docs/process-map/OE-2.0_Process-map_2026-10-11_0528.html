Organizational Excellence · OE 2.0

From people protecting performance to systems protecting performance

The aim
Close the loop. Every finding, from a shift checklist, an audit or a guest concern, gets one owner, a due date and a confirmed fix. Leaders see it the next morning in the Excellence Gateway.
"We can't measure what we can't observe, and we can't manage what we can't measure."
OE slogan, 2014
Strengthen Assign and Follow upToday's cycle is strong at Capture and Measure, and thin in between.
One list, one standardStandardized processes instead of WhatsApp, paper and personal habits.
Honest numbersScores that show what needs action, read straight from the work itself.
Same language everywhereFrom the room attendant's phone to the morning briefing and the OKR report.
AreaWhat we are fixing (today)What we gain (OE 2.0)
Follow-upFollow-up on problems runs on WhatsApp and paper. Nothing shows what is still open.→One problem list for every finding, with a photo, one owner and a due date. Overdue shows automatically.
AuditsAudits are done on paper and typed up later. The due-date column is empty on every quality sheet; no owners are named.→OE audits on the phone, with photos. Any finding becomes an action with an owner and a due date on the spot.
Repeated findingsFindings come back ("Repeated", "despite general cleaning"). Orders are marked done without the work.→A fix counts only when someone else confirms it. Places with repeated problems are flagged.
Shift standardsMost teams have no shift checklist. Audits note "no closing check".→A short shift checklist for each place, opened by QR code and checked by the supervisor.
Guest concernsDepartmental Concerns and Exclusivi alerts reach the department fast, but the action is not tracked to closure.→"Add as a problem" turns a guest concern into a tracked action in the same list.
HSSESerious safety findings can sit outside the score ("Max 0").→Safety first: always at the top of every list, needs a temporary measure, open count seen by the GM and Owner.
OKR scorecardToo forgiving to steer by: "On Track" from 90%. Water over its limit still reads On Track.→Four clear statuses: Met, Close, Behind, At risk. A limit is read as a limit.
ReportsData is built by hand in spreadsheets: typed-over values, #REF! cells, labels that differ between files.→Numbers come straight from the work done in the app. No retyping; the morning briefing runs on today's data.
Bonuses (next step)Department scores include resort rows copied onto every sheet, and guest scores partly shaped by who is asked to review.→A draft scorecard of a few outcome KPIs per department, from high-integrity sources only. Never counts of problems reported or checklists ticked.

What stays the same: the Excellence Gateway, its four pillars (Financial, Operational, Guest experience, People & Culture), the Morning Briefing, OKRs, Exclusivi alerts and the monthly reports. OE 2.0 adds the missing link between finding a problem and fixing it.

CAPTURE

Where findings come from

Shift checklistsLine staff: pool & beach attendant, room attendantScan the QR code at the place. Tick each item or mark "could not do". Report a problem from any item, with a photo.
OE auditsQuality team, on the moveTwo marks per item: steps followed, and result good. Any finding becomes a problem, with the note and photo carried over.
Guest concernsDepartmental Concerns, Exclusivi, review platformsIn the Morning Briefing, "Add as a problem" fills in the place, type and the guest's words.
Seen on the spotAnyone, including the MOD roundWhat, where, a photo, condition or care, and whether it is a safety risk.

Back into the loop: anything raised in a briefing or by a leader is added as a problem, with an owner and a due date.   Rules: safety first · one owner per problem · no one confirms their own fix · overdue is automatic · counts by place, never by person · Arabic and English.
