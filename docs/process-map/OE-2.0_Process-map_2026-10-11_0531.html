Organizational Excellence · OE 2.0

From people protecting performance to systems protecting performance

The aim
Every finding, whether it comes from a shift checklist, an audit or a guest, should end with one owner, a due date and a fix that someone else has actually checked. Leaders should then see where it stands the next morning, in the Excellence Gateway.
"We can't measure what we can't observe, and we can't manage what we can't measure."
OE slogan, 2014
Strengthen Assign and Follow upWe are already good at capturing and measuring. The weak part is what happens in between.
One list, one standardToday follow-up lives in WhatsApp groups, on paper and in people's heads, so it depends on who is on shift.
Honest numbersScores should show what needs action, and come straight from the work rather than from someone retyping it.
The same words everywhereThe room attendant's phone, the Morning Briefing and the OKR report should all call things by the same name.
AreaWhat we are fixing (today)What we gain (OE 2.0)
Follow-upProblems get followed up on WhatsApp and on paper, which means nobody can really say what is still open.→One list for every problem, each with a photo, one owner and a due date. If it slips past the due date, it shows as overdue without anyone having to chase it.
AuditsAudits are done on paper and typed up later. The due-date column is empty on every quality sheet, and nobody is named as owner.→OE audits happen on the phone, with photos, and any finding can become an action with an owner and a due date right there.
Repeated findingsThe same findings keep coming back ("Repeated", "despite general cleaning"), and some orders get marked done when the work wasn't.→A fix only counts once someone other than the person who did it has checked it. Places where problems keep coming back get flagged.
Shift standardsMost teams don't have a shift checklist, and the audits often say "no closing check".→Each place has a short shift checklist, opened with a QR code and checked by the supervisor.
Guest concernsDepartmental Concerns and Exclusivi alerts reach the department quickly, but what happens after that isn't really tracked.→"Add as a problem" turns a guest concern into an action in the same list, so we can see when it is actually fixed.
HSSESome serious safety findings sit outside the score ("Max 0"), so they are easy to miss.→Safety problems always sit at the top of every list and need a temporary measure first, and the GM and Owner can see how many are open.
OKR scorecardIt is quite forgiving. "On Track" starts at 90%, and water use reads On Track even when it is over the limit.→Four clear statuses (Met, Close, Behind, At risk), and a limit is treated as a limit.
ReportsA lot of the data is built by hand in spreadsheets, with typed-over values, #REF! cells and labels that change from file to file.→The numbers come from the work people actually do in the app, so nobody retypes anything and the Morning Briefing runs on today's data.
Bonuses (next step)Department scores include resort rows copied onto every sheet, and some guest scores depend on who we ask to leave a review.→A draft scorecard with a few outcome KPIs per department, taken only from sources we trust. I think it should never count problems reported or checklists ticked, because that rewards hiding problems.

What stays the same: the Excellence Gateway and its four pillars (Financial, Operational, Guest experience, People & Culture), the Morning Briefing, OKRs, Exclusivi alerts and the monthly reports. None of that goes away. OE 2.0 adds the part that is missing today, which is the link between finding a problem and actually fixing it.

Orange is where the OE app does the work, and teal is where the Excellence Gateway does it. It is the same management cycle we use today.

CAPTURE

Where findings come from

Shift checklistsLine staff: pool & beach attendant, room attendantStaff scan the QR code at the place and tick each item, or mark it "could not do". They can report a problem from any item, with a photo.
OE auditsQuality team, on the moveEach item gets two marks: were the steps followed, and is the result good? Any finding can become a problem, and the note and photo come with it.
Guest concernsDepartmental Concerns, Exclusivi, review platformsIn the Morning Briefing, "Add as a problem" fills in the place, the type and the guest's own words.
Seen on the spotAnyone, including the MOD roundWhat it is, where, a photo, whether it is condition or care, and whether it is a safety risk.
↓ EVERYTHING GOES INTO ONE PROBLEM LIST, WITH ONE SET OF RULES ↓

The problem loop

ReportedPlace, photo and type. If it is a safety risk, a temporary measure comes first.
→

→

MEASURE · LEARN

Who looks at what, and when

SupervisorThrough the shiftChecklists to confirm, fixes to confirm and problems to assign.
→
DirectorEvery morningThe department overview: safety, today's checklists, fixes nobody has checked yet, how long problems have waited and where they keep clustering.
→
Morning BriefingGM and each department, dailyOne page per department, with KPIs against target, today in the OE app, yesterday's and MTD guest concerns, and what needs discussing.
→
GM, CEO, OwnerGM daily · CEO daily and weekly · Owner weeklySafety, what needs a push, the weekly trend and the CAPEX list.
→
Excellence GatewayMonthly OKR cycleThe four pillars, each KPI against target (Met, Close, Behind, At risk), and a draft scorecard for future bonuses.

Back into the loop: anything raised in a briefing or by a leader is added as a problem with an owner and a due date, and followed up like everything else. The rules: safety first, one owner per problem, nobody confirms their own fix, overdue happens on its own, and we count by place, never by person. In Arabic and English.
