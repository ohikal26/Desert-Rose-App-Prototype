From prototype to a system Desert Rose can run on

The recommendation
Build our own system on top of the prototype, with a development partner, and keep the Excellence Gateway in Power BI. Before we commit, run a short, fair test of the two best alternatives, because the choice locks us in for years.

Why not simply buy a tool?

We looked at the main hotel and audit apps, and none of them could be confirmed to run fully in Arabic, right to left. SafetyCulture, GoAudits and HotSOS list no Arabic interface at all. hotelkit and Flexkeeping machine-translate the content, but the buttons and menus stay in English. Most of our line staff read Arabic far better than English, so I think that alone rules them out as the main tool.

The regional products we found are mostly hotel PMS or ERP systems, which would mean replacing Opera. That's a much bigger decision than this project needs.

Why build, and what we'd test against it

The prototype already shows the full Arabic app working, with our own rules (safety first, nobody confirms their own fix, overdue happens on its own). Roughly a third to half of the visible app carries over. What's missing is everything behind it: a server, sign-in, shared data and safe syncing.

The two alternatives worth a fair test are low-code platforms with Arabic built in: Microsoft Power Apps, because we already run the Gateway on Power BI, and Zoho Creator, which documents a mirrored right-to-left layout. Page 2 has the detail.

What success looks like after year one

  • Every department runs its shift checklists and problems in the app, in Arabic or English.
  • Overdue problems and repeated audit findings are clearly down against today's baseline.
  • No safety problem sits open without a temporary measure and an owner.
  • The Morning Briefing runs on live numbers, and nobody retypes data into spreadsheets.

Decisions we need now

  • A budget range for year one, and whether a yearly running cost is acceptable.
  • Who in OE owns the system, and who on the leadership side signs off at each gate.
  • Permission to approach two or three development partners and run the comparison on page 2.
  • A target date. I'd aim for two departments live before the summer peak of 2027.

Power Apps, checked properly

  • Offline works only in the native Power Apps mobile app (not a browser) and only with Dataverse data, with some limits on filters and relationships.
  • Microsoft documents RTL for Arabic on some page types. For canvas apps, users report a mirrored layout on the Windows app and say there is no reliable fix yet. Phones need a real test.
  • Licences are per named user: Premium is about USD 20 a month, or about USD 10 per active user per app on pay-as-you-go. Shared team phones need checking against the licence terms.

The comparison test (2–3 weeks)

Each shortlisted route builds the same small slice: the pool opening checklist, one problem reported with a photo, assigned, fixed and confirmed by someone else, all in Arabic, on a phone, at the beach with weak Wi-Fi. We score Arabic, offline, speed for staff, our rules, cost over 3 years, and who owns the result. Custom build, Power Apps and Zoho Creator go into the test.

What happens in each phase

0  Decide and set up

4 weeks
  • Name the system owner in OE and a sponsor in leadership.
  • Shortlist 2–3 development partners (an Egyptian agency keeps cost and time zone right).
  • Contract terms: we own the code, data and documentation; a second firm can take over.
  • Hosting on Azure (we already use Microsoft) and a data-protection check under Law 151.

Done when: Owner named, partners shortlisted, contract terms agreed.

1  Staff pilot and comparison test

3–4 weeks
  • Supervisors use the prototype for real in Recreation and Housekeeping.
  • We write the fixed must-have list for release 1 from what they actually use.
  • Run the comparison test on page 2 and score the results.

Done when: Must-have list, scored comparison, Gate 1 decision.

2  Build release 1

about 16 weeks
  • Sign-in by role; shared phones use a PIN or badge.
  • Central database, photo store, and a full record of who did what and when.
  • Offline syncing that copes with two people changing the same problem.
  • An admin screen so OE changes checklists without a developer.
  • Alerts for safety and overdue problems (badge, push or WhatsApp).
  • Security test, automatic backups, uptime monitoring.

Done when: Release 1 tested on real devices at the pool, beach and back-of-house.

3  Live pilot

8 weeks
  • Recreation and Housekeeping run on the new system, with paper as a backup for the first weeks.
  • Weekly fixes; measure against today: overdue problems, repeated findings, days to fix.

Done when: Pilot report and Gate 2 decision.

4  Rollout and Gateway live

June, then Sep–Dec
  • One or two departments a month, each with its own checklists and training. No go-lives in the July and August peak.
  • Data flows into Power BI, so the Morning Briefing runs on live numbers.
  • Then the outside links, one at a time: Exclusivi, TrustYou, and later Opera.

Done when: Whole resort live; Morning Briefing on live data.

5  Run and improve

ongoing
  • A clear first contact for problems, with fast cover in high season.
  • Monthly updates and a short upgrade list owned by OE.
  • A yearly review of cost, use and what to improve next.

Done when: A system that keeps getting better instead of drifting.

What makes it robust

  • Works offline, and syncs safely when the signal comes back.
  • Wi-Fi where the work happens, not only in offices.
  • Devices with a home: charged, shared by team, stored in one place between shifts.
  • One owner who changes settings; departments don't change them alone.
  • Backups, monitoring and a support line that answers at 7am in August.
  • Data protection: clear rules on who sees staff names and photos, and where data is stored.
  • Upgradable by design: open API, full data export, and checklists changed by OE, not by code.

Risks, the next 30 days, and sources

Main risks

RiskLevelHow we handle it
Depending on one partner or one developerMediumWe own the code; documentation and automated tests are part of the contract; a second firm can take over.
Scope keeps growingHighRelease 1 is two departments and the core loop only. Everything else waits for the gates.
Offline syncing is harder than expectedMediumBuild and test it first, in the real places, before anything else.
Staff don't use it every shiftMediumSupervisors help shape it in phase 1; champions per department; the first weeks get extra support.

The next 30 days

  1. Leadership agrees the route in principle, a budget range and the system owner.
  2. Book the staff pilot of the prototype with Recreation and Housekeeping supervisors.

  4. Survey Wi-Fi at the pools, beach and back-of-house.
